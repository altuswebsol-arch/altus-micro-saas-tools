## Body copy (BELOW the tool)

### Headline: What Google shows is what gets clicked.

Your meta description is the 155 characters sitting under your page title in Google's results. It doesn't directly change your ranking — but it decides whether anyone clicks. A clear, honest description with the searcher's words in it wins the click; a missing, cut-off, or robotic one hands it to the competitor below you.

The awkward part: you can't just count characters. Google cuts descriptions by pixel width, not by character — a line full of wide letters (W, M) gets cut much earlier than a line of narrow ones (i, l, t). And if your description is missing, too short, or reads like keyword stuffing, Google simply writes its own from your page text — which may say nothing like what you wanted.

That's what this tool checks. Paste a URL and it reads the live page, or paste your own draft text. You get:

- A score out of 100, in plain English — what passes, what needs work, and exactly how to fix it.
- A live Google preview — desktop and mobile — cut where Google would cut it, so you see the truncation before your visitors do.
- Pixel-width measurement — the real cut-off, estimated from font metrics, not just a character count.
- Keyword check — is your target phrase in there, and is it near the front where it survives truncation?
- Call-to-action check — does it tell the searcher what to do next? ("Book", "Shop", "Get a quote" beat silence.)
- Shouting and copy-paste detection — all-caps words and descriptions identical to the title both read badly in results.

### Does the meta description affect my Google ranking?
Not directly — Google has said it isn't a ranking factor. But it strongly affects your click-through rate: the words searchers see decide whether they choose your listing or the one below it. More clicks on the same ranking means more business from the traffic you already have.

### Why does Google rewrite my meta description?
Google rewrites descriptions it considers unhelpful — usually because they're missing, very short, stuffed with keywords, or identical across many pages. Writing a unique, honest 120–155 character description for each important page is the best way to keep control of what searchers see.

### How long should a meta description be?
Aim for 120–155 characters, and keep the important words in the first 120. Google cuts by pixel width (about 920 pixels on desktop), not characters — wide letters eat more space. The tool measures both and shows you exactly where the cut lands.

### Are the pixel widths exact?
They're close estimates based on Arial's font metrics, calibrated to Google's typical cut-off. Google renders snippets dynamically and sometimes shows different text, so treat the preview as a faithful approximation — not a pixel-perfect guarantee.

### Can I check a competitor's page?
Yes — paste any public URL. It's a handy way to see how your snippet stacks up against the listings around you.
